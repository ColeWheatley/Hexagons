01 · THE LAYERS

Three views of the same mountain

Tap any point on the map and you get all three at once — the layers below are how that same underlying simulation gets colored onto the terrain.

Snow quality

What it means: a 0–100 index blending modeled depth, density and surface age at each cell. Pink is the good stuff.

WHAT IT ISN'T A slope-safety score. Deep pink powder can sit on a dangerous snowpack.

Snow depth

What it means: modeled snow depth in centimetres at every 17 m cell, updated hourly with the rest of the model.

WHAT IT ISN'T A measurement. No sensor has been there — it's the model's estimate.

Avalanche susceptibility

What it means: a modeled release-susceptibility class and a runout estimate, from the same snowpack physics as the other two layers.

WHAT IT ISN'T A forecast or a danger rating. Not the LWD bulletin. See below.

02 · READ THIS FIRST

This is a model. It is not a forecast.

WHAT IT IS

A physics-based snowpack simulation, forced hourly by GeoSphere Austria's INCA analysis and run over a 17-metre terrain mesh of the Stubai Alps. Every layer on this page — quality, depth, avalanche susceptibility — comes out of that same simulation.

WHAT IT IS NOT

Not a weather forecast, not a field observation, and not an avalanche bulletin. It has never been validated against station or field data. PowFinder does not predict avalanche release — susceptibility is a modeled indicator, not a prediction. A "considerable" cell can be safe; a "low" one can slide.

04 · HOW IT WORKS

Three ideas, stacked

1

17-metre hexagons, everywhere

The whole Stubai range subdivided into a fractal hex grid — the same terrain the engine already draws at 60 fps, now carrying snow data too.

2

A 1-D snowpack model, per cell

Every hex runs its own hourly snowpack simulation, forced by the same INCA weather analysis that drives the whole model.

3

Rendered at 60 fps, on a phone

Conditions ride the same terrain engine that draws the mountain — no separate map layer, no extra load.
